A rice mill in Kedah produces husk faster than anyone can use it. It piles up behind the dryer until someone sets it alight, and a season of captured carbon goes back into the air in an afternoon. Multiply that by every mill, estate and smallholder from the Peninsula to Sulawesi.

That residue is not waste. It is feedstock. Heated without oxygen it becomes biochar, and the carbon the plant pulled down last season stops being temporary. We build the kilns, measure what comes out of them, and make the removal bankable — on the same ground the biomass came from.

The problem

Carbon finance was built for
balance sheets,
not for farmland.

Carbon removal works. Access to it does not. Project development costs six figures before the first tonne is verified, methodologies are written for consultants, and registries expect documentation a smallholder cooperative has no way to produce.

So the market consolidates around a handful of large operators, and the communities living with the emissions — the ones with the biomass, the land and the most to gain — are priced out of the thing designed to pay them.

  • 01 Residue burns because disposal costs money and biochar infrastructure does not exist locally.
  • 02 MRV is manual, slow and expensive, so buyers discount the credit before it is issued.
  • 03 Revenue stops at the aggregator. Very little reaches the farm gate.

The method

Pyrolysis, measured
at every stage.

Biochar is only a removal if you can prove it stayed put. Five controlled stages turn loose residue into a carbon lattice soil microbes cannot easily break down — each one instrumented, sampled and written to the registry record.

  1. 01Additionality

    Feedstock sourcing

    Palm EFB, rice husk and forestry residue that would otherwise decompose or burn. If the biomass had another life, it does not qualify.

    Eligible streams EFB · husk · forestry

  2. 02Preparation

    Sizing & drying

    Mechanical processing to a uniform particle size, then solar and waste-heat drying until moisture falls below the pyrolysis threshold.

    Moisture < 10%

  3. 03Conversion

    Pyrolysis

    Held in a low-oxygen chamber at target temperature. Higher, steadier heat builds a more stable chemical lattice and a higher fixed-carbon fraction.

    Target 600–900 °C

  4. 04Stabilisation

    Quench & inoculate

    Immediate water quenching arrests the reaction and fixes the structure. Nutrient inoculation happens here, before the char leaves the site.

    Cooling Immediate quench

  5. 05Verification

    Elemental analysis

    Lab analysis against the durability benchmark confirms carbon stable beyond a century. Verified removals are issued on a global registry.

    Permanence 100+ years

Biochar is recognised as a durable removal pathway by the IPCC, the EU CRCF, the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles and SBTi.
