The origin story

Terra preta
of the Amazon.

Centuries ago, Indigenous communities in the Amazon enriched poor soils with charred organic matter, creating fertile terra preta that still thrives today. Nobody was counting tonnes of CO₂ — they were trying to grow food on ground that would not cooperate.

That practice is the foundation of modern biochar. What changed is the measurement around it: the same charred carbon, now characterised in a lab, tracked to a registry and priced.

What is biochar?

A carbon-rich material produced by heating biomass — crop residues from agriculture, or wood waste — in a low-oxygen process called pyrolysis. Unlike ordinary charcoal, biochar is made for environmental and agricultural use: it locks carbon into solid form instead of releasing it as CO₂.

Sustainable feedstock sourcing

For true carbon removal the biomass must meet the additionality criteria — the material (palm EFB, rice husks, forestry residues) would otherwise have rotted or been burned, releasing CO₂ and methane.

Our collection process emphasises traceability and ecological balance. We only gather residues that are not required for local soil health, so the surrounding ecosystem stays net-positive rather than quietly stripped to feed a kiln.
